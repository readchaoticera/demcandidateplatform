How to read this

  • Position combines three kinds of evidence, by precedence. Cosponsoring H.R.3069 — a recorded legislative act — outranks a candidate’s own campaign site, which outranks news coverage. Every row records which one decided it, and the campaign-site-only reading is shown inside the row for comparison.
  • “Does not support” means no Medicare for All position was found anywhere. That covers three different things, and only the first is a stated contrary position: a candidate who backs a public option, ACA measures or outright opposition; one whose material was read and states nothing about how coverage should work; and one whose site could not be read at all. The last two are inferences from silence, and the third is not evidence about the candidate. Open any row to see which applies — the finer seven-tier classification is kept per candidate, and in the CSV.
  • A sitting member has to have signed the bill. Cosponsoring H.R.3069 costs a member nothing but says everything, so for incumbents the Medicare for All tiers require it. Where a campaign line or a press profile calls a member a supporter and their name is not on the bill, the claim is dropped rather than replaced — whatever else their own material says stands. Challengers have no bill to sign and are not held to this.
  • The gap between the two measures is the story. Most cosponsors never mention the bill on their own campaign site, so the site-only count is under half the combined one.
  • Districts may carry several candidates. Louisiana runs an all-party ballot with no nominating primary, and California, Washington and Alaska can send more than one Democrat to the same general election. Those are not duplicates.
  • Some sites serve a crawler almost nothing. Where a campaign runs a Javascript-rendered site, those rows are corrected by hand, recorded with a source and a named reviewer, and marked human review.
  • The Cook rating describes the district, not the candidate. It is The Cook Political Report’s judgement of how competitive the seat is, reproduced here for context via Wikipedia’s ratings tables, which cite Cook for each one. It never feeds the position classification, and a safe seat is not evidence of anything about what its candidate believes.
